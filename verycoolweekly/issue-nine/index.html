Issue Nine

Designing In Iran · The New Twitter · Designing a Brand in VR

From the 2017 archive. These picks reflect their original publication date; external sites and offers may have changed.

Welcome to Issue Nine of Very Cool Weekly.

I've put together a nice mix of design and development articles for you this week. We've got something near and dear to my heart: Iranian design. There's a breakdown of the new Twitter design, how to VR-first brand, a VERY FUN typography game, a technical article on how Instagram upgraded their platform serving 400,000,000 users per day, and why I switched from using tabs to using spaces in my code.

I'd also like to ask that if you're enjoying what you're reading, please consider sharing @verycoolweekly on Twitter. It really goes a long way!

Editorial pick · via Tobias Van Schneider

Designing In Iran

As half-Iranian, this article from Tobias Van Schneider really struck a chord with me. Typically, modern Iran is perceived as very oppressive and not conducive to the arts. In an interview with Studio Melli, it's clear that some Iranian designers are working hard to buck that stereotype.

Editorial pick · via The New Stack

Instagram Makes a Smooth Move to Python 3

400,000,000 users a day. That's an intimidating number. Consider how the Instagram team felt when they decided to upgrade their entire platform. Shivers. Instagram engineers Hui Ding and Lisa Guo talk about not only the technical details behind the migration, but also some of the higher-level decisions behind moving forward with such a massive project.

Editorial pick · via Stack Overflow Blog

Developers Who Use Spaces Make More Money Than Those Who Use Tabs

So I use tabs. After reading this article on Stack Overflow, I think I'm going to switch over to using spaces. You know, because it's more detailed. Right. That's definitely why. Make sure to read through some of the comments after the article. My favorite? "WHAT IF PEOPLE WHO USE SPACES ARE PAID BY THE HOUR AND TAKE LONGER TO TYPE STUFF?"
